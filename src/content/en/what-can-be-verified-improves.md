A few months ago I wrote an essay answering a question I've been asked a lot over the past six months. AI changes every week. You play with one agent framework today, and a few months later there's a new one. Where exactly should we invest our time? The answer I gave back then was to chase tools less. Instead, take your workflows, the pitfalls you've encountered, and the criteria you base decisions on—things that truly belong to you—and organize them into memory that an AI can read.

Recently I wrote another piece. It's another realization from the same period, and the theme is verifiability.

For the past few months, I've been running this memory-building approach in my own workflow. I found that while the outputs got more complete, I still made it a habit to read the whole thing again before using it. I didn't actually save much time. Later I realized where I was stuck: everything I stuffed into the memory was about "how to do it," but what I was actually checking was "does this count as a good job." The AI had context, but it didn't have my acceptance criteria.

Then I happened to read a question from Kai-Fu Lee: why did AI coding take off so early in this wave of AI? Why programming, of all things?

He said: because code can be verified.

This sounds simple at first glance. But the more I thought about it, the more I felt that what it implies goes far beyond just writing code.

Think about it. We dare to let AI clean up transcripts, but we're hesitant to let it reply to client emails. The difficulty of these two tasks is actually about the same. The difference is that if the transcript is wrong, you know it at a glance. If the client email is wrong, you only find out from their reaction, and by then it's too late.

Where you deploy AI actually reflects the boundaries of your own verification ability. It has little to do with the AI's ability.

Following this thread of "acceptance criteria," I finally put together this essay. Thanks to Muyueh Lee for inviting me to write these two pieces. Honestly, if there hadn't been a deadline forcing me, these thoughts probably would have stayed stuck in my head indefinitely 😂
