I'm honored to be a speaker at the 2026 Taiwan AI Annual Conference this year~
My talk is titled "From AI Sessions to Team Memory: The Design and Implementation of Cairn."

This topic is actually something I started at the Anthropic Hackathon and have kept iterating on ever since.

In the year-plus I've spent building AILogora, one question has stayed with me. These days, when people hit a bug, they just open Cursor or Claude, ask, fix it, and close the window. The judgment calls and experience that made that debugging valuable disappear the moment the session ends.

The result: the same pothole your AI fell into yesterday, your coworker's AI falls into all over again today.

Cairn is my attempt to fix that. It takes the records of potholes and architecture decisions scattered across individual AI sessions and turns them into a memory layer the whole team can share. In this talk I'll go through our current design thinking, the problems we've run into running it for real, and how an agent can help you organize knowledge "without wiping out the context."
